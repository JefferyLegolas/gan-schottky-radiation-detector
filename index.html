Engineering GaN Schottky Diodes for Space Radiation Detection

A quasi-vertical GaN Schottky barrier diode study comparing SiO₂-passivated and unpassivated devices from 300–500 K through forward I–V, C–V and reverse-current transport analysis.

Tianli Shi · Final-year research project · Supervisor: Ivona Mitrovic
Study boundary

Device readiness, not radiation qualification.

The work establishes the electrical behaviour of a candidate GaN detector structure and the trade-off introduced by SiO₂ passivation. It does not report particle exposure, detector sensitivity or energy resolution.

DeviceQuasi-vertical GaN SBD

No radiation test was executed. “For space applications” refers to design potential and the next validation stage—not demonstrated in-orbit detector performance.

Core result

Passivation improves the barrier, but charges a conduction penalty.

At the reported comparison points, SiO₂ raises the turn-on voltage, on-resistance and extracted Schottky barrier height, while leaving donor concentration and energy-level separation nearly unchanged.

Device architecture

A compact quasi-vertical current path.

The structure combines vertical-field handling in the drift region with top-side contacts, making the device a practical bridge between lateral and fully vertical GaN designs.

Evidence chain

Three measurements build one device story.

Forward transport quantifies the passivation penalty; capacitance probes the underlying electronic structure; reverse transport points to trap-assisted conduction.

Evidence boundaries

What the project establishes—and what remains open.

The value of the study is strongest when its conclusions remain tightly aligned with the measurements actually performed.

Established

Electrical feasibility of the candidate structure

The study characterises quasi-vertical GaN SBD behaviour, quantifies the SiO₂ trade-off and extracts a plausible reverse-current mechanism.

Not yet established

Radiation detector performance

No proton, neutron, gamma or alpha exposure was performed; sensitivity, charge-collection efficiency, energy resolution and radiation degradation remain unmeasured.

Experimental limit

Single-sample evidence

The thesis notes that results came from one sample and that low-voltage, 300–350 K and selected reverse-bias data contained appreciable noise.

Next test

Radiation-before/after comparison

Future work should measure I–V and C–V shifts, leakage, barrier height and long-term stability under controlled particle fluence.
